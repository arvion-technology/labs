Where product work breaks

The decision is made long before the pixels arrive.

By the time a designer opens a file, the scope has usually been negotiated twice and the person who understood the customer is in another meeting. Most of our work is closing that gap.

A / Scope

“Can you also add…”

A fixed discovery finds the expensive unknowns before they become a change request. The output is a scope both sides can defend.

B / Handover

Designs that stop at the mockup

Every component ships with states, copy and a note on the behaviour. Engineers should not have to invent the loading state.

C / Momentum

The weekly demo that isn't

We demo the actual artefact every week. If there is nothing to show, that is information, not something to manage.

Services

Three ways in, all fixed-scope.

01

Product design sprint

Two weeks from a working hypothesis to a clickable core flow. You get the flow, the states, and a decision on what to cut.

02

Design system

Tokens, components and a documentation page your engineers can use without asking us. Built in your stack, not ours.

03

Mobile app interface

Flutter or React Native screens with real content constraints, empty states and the offline path designed before launch.

Approach

A month looks like this.

One call and a written brief

You answer five questions by email. We reply with a scope, a fixed fee and a date.

Week one: the real content

We work from your copy, data and constraints, not from filler text. If the content does not exist, we design the state where it does not.

Week two: build the core flow

You see a working prototype midweek. We revise what the demo exposes, not what we imagine it will.

Handover: files, notes, walkthrough

Source files, component notes and a recorded walkthrough for your engineers. The repo is yours; no licence to negotiate.

The engagement is the proof

You can check these on day two.

We do not publish client logos. What we can commit to is how the work is run, and that is visible from the first week.

A weekly artefact

Not a status update. A screen, a prototype or a written decision, every Friday.

A shared repository

Work lands in your repo or a shared workspace from day one, so handover is a permission change, not a migration.

One point of contact

The person you brief is the person doing the work. There is no layer between the decision and the file.

Questions

What founders ask on the first call.

The answers are short because the engagement is short. Nothing here is a retainer.

How do you price?

A fixed fee per sprint, agreed before it starts. If the scope changes, we send a new fee and you accept or decline; there is no hourly drift.

Can you work with our existing team?

Yes, that is the usual shape. We join standups, design in your repo and write components your engineers maintain afterwards. We do not require a design team of your own.

Who owns the files?

You do, on payment of the sprint. Figma files, source, notes and the walkthrough are handed over. We ask for permission before showing any work publicly, and no is a complete answer.

Tell us what you are building. This week.

One email, five questions, a written scope and a fixed fee. If we are not the right studio, we will say so in the reply.
